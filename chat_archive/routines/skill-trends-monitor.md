Monitor and summarize emerging trends in human skills, AI disruption, and learning preferences.

1. Scan recent articles, reports, and job market data on AI's impact on workforce skills and employment.
Human/"durable" skills: Critical thinking, problem-solving, decision-making, collaboration, communication, leadership, emotional intelligence, and debugging/validation ("human-in-the-loop" oversight of AI outputs). These rank highly for current and future success, as AI automates routine tasks but amplifies the need for judgment. 
Niche/experiential learning: Languages, culinary skills, wellness, crafts, or sustainable living—especially via travel or "skillidays" (popular with Gen Z). Gamified learning, homework help tools, and personal development (e.g., well-being) also trend

2. Identify and categorize trends into: durable human skills (critical thinking, leadership, emotional intelligence), niche/experiential learning (languages, wellness, crafts, travel-based learning), and AI-driven job market shifts (wage premiums, skill obsolescence risks).
3. Note any new data on skill demand growth rates, wage premiums for AI skills, or worker sentiment about job security.
4. Compile a brief summary with key findings, sources, and any notable shifts from previous reports.

In short, people are learning to stay relevant in an AI-augmented world, boost employability/productivity, and build versatile, future-proof capabilities. The focus is increasingly on actionable combinations (e.g., AI + critical thinking + domain expertise) rather than isolated topics. Trends evolve quickly, so learning how to learn and adapting fast remains the meta-skill.
