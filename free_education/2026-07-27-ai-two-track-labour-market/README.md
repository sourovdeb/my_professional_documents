## 1. The new lead: it's demand now, not just pay

The July premium number (62%) held — but the **volume** number is the fresh story.
PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer (1 billion+ postings, 27 countries) reports
US job postings requiring AI skills grew **144% year-on-year** as of this spring,
against **7%** for postings overall. *SUCCESS* summarised it bluntly: **"62% more
pay, 144% more demand."** Jobs needing AI skills are now growing roughly **8×
faster** than the market.

**Why it matters:** a premium tells you a skill is *valued*. A demand curve like
that tells you it's becoming *table stakes* — the way "can use a spreadsheet"
stopped being a bragging right and became an assumption.

---

## 2. The premium isn't one number — it's a spread

The single "+62%" hides a widening gap. PwC's new cut shows the premium reaching
**~118% in consumer-facing roles** and collapsing to **~16% in government and
public-sector** jobs. PwC's own framing for the 2026 edition is a **"two-track
labour market."**

The lesson: *where* you apply AI + judgment now matters as much as *whether* you do.

## 3. The one that should make you sit up: AI is eating the junior on-ramp

This is the genuinely new structural finding. AI removes the routine work that
**used to be the apprenticeship** — the grunt tasks juniors did to learn the
trade — while pushing demand for **judgment, leadership and adaptability much
earlier** in a career. PwC finds entry-level AI-exposed roles are **~7× more
likely** to require traditionally senior skills.

**Real example.** A first-year analyst in 2022 spent months reconciling
spreadsheets — dull, but it taught them what "wrong data" *feels* like. In 2026
the reconciliation is done by an agent before they arrive at the desk. The job ad
for that same seat now asks them to *validate the agent's output and brief a
director* on day one — a judgment task that used to take three years to earn.

The on-ramp didn't disappear; it moved **up the wall**. The practical takeaway
for anyone early-career or re-entering: don't try to compete on the routine layer
AI already owns — build the validation-and-judgment layer *deliberately*, because
the job market now expects it from the start.

## 4. Durable skills: critical thinking is now the *fastest-growing* one

The 19 July brief reported critical thinking as the **#1 skill leaders want**.
The fresher framing gives it a velocity: the Job Skills Report 2026 finds
**triple-digit year-over-year growth in Critical Thinking across every single
cohort** — its authors call it "perhaps the most important finding in the report."
The Critical Core Skills 2026 framework now files 16 human skills under three
clusters — **Thinking Critically · Interacting with Others · Staying Relevant** —
and flags a new sub-category outright: **debugging, data-quality and responsible-AI
oversight** as non-negotiable "human-in-the-loop" skills.

## 5. The mood: satisfaction is starting to crack

The background anxiety from July hasn't eased — it's spreading into satisfaction.
- **"Extremely satisfied"** workers fell **49.7% → 41.7% in a single quarter**
  (4 Corner Resources Q2 2026) — a 16% relative drop in the strongest tier.
- Only **22%** of workers globally still strongly agree their job is safe (ADP,
  unchanged) — the economy (**42%**) is still cited as a bigger threat than AI
  (**17%**), but AI is climbing.
- **Anxiety is now inverted by seniority:** executives report the highest AI
  threat concern (**30.8%**, ~2.5× any other stage), and among tech researchers
  **51%** are anxious about job security vs **15%** of founders.
- AI mentions in employee reviews are up **240% YoY**, and sentiment has **flipped
  from 55% positive a year ago to 53% negative**.

Fear remains a poor strategy but a useful signal: it's the market telling people
to move from *doing the task* to *owning the judgment* — exactly Track A.

## 6. The counter-current: people are voting with their holidays (and now booking)

The "skilliday" is maturing from intention into behaviour.
- Nearly **half of Europeans** plan to learn a skill on holiday (Mastercard,
  27,000 travellers / 28 countries) — and **37% have already booked** one.
- **82%** say learning a skill while travelling makes the trip more memorable;
  **79%** of Gen Z + Millennials plan to seek out local workshops.
- Top picks: **languages (30%), cooking with local chefs (28%), food & drink
  production — winemaking, cheesemaking (28%).**

Same instinct as the workplace shift: reach for something **embodied, feedback-
driven and hard to automate**. Pottery and negotiation are closer cousins than
they look.

## 🛠 A tool to try this week

**The Two-Track Self-Audit** (10 minutes, one page):

1. **List** the 5 tasks that fill most of your week.
2. **Sort** each into **Track B** (routine — AI can already draft it) or **Track A**
   (needs your judgment, taste, or trust).
3. **Count your Track B tasks.** That share is your automation exposure — and,
   right now, your *displacement risk*.
4. For each Track-B task, write the **Track-A judgment** it frees you for
   (validate the output · decide what not to do · persuade a human).
5. Pick **one** durable skill (critical thinking, a validation habit, a
   leadership rep) and **one** refreshable technical skill. Set a **90-day review**
   — beat the skill half-life on purpose.

**Pair it with a real, free tool.** Use any AI assistant as a **sparring partner,
not an oracle**: after you reach a conclusion, paste it back and prompt —
> *"Argue the strongest case that I'm wrong. List the three assumptions I didn't check."*

That single habit converts the AI from an autopilot into a critical-thinking gym —
the exact Track-A muscle the 2026 market is repricing upward. (See the accompanying
`mindmap.mm` for a full SCAMPER/inversion brainstorm of Track-A moves, and the
`.pptx` doodle deck for a shareable 9-slide version.)
