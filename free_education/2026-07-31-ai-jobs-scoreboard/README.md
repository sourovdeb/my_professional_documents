## 1. The layoff ledger now names AI — repeatedly

The genuinely new signal is in **Challenger, Gray & Christmas's** monthly job-cut
report, which tags every announced layoff with the employer's *stated* reason.

- AI was the **#1 stated reason for job cuts for the fourth consecutive month** (June 2026).
- **101,743 job cuts** were attributed to AI in the first half of 2026 — **nearly double the 54,836** attributed to it in *all of 2025*.
- June alone: **14,029 AI-cited cuts = 31%** of the month's total.
- Tech led every sector: **139,156 cuts in H1, +83% year-on-year** — "companies are restructuring *around* AI."

A caveat worth keeping: "stated reason" is what companies *say*, and some AI mentions
are cover for ordinary cost-cutting. But four straight months at #1 is a pattern, not
a headline. Economists at **Goldman Sachs** read it as AI **suppressing hiring more than
firing** — the damage shows up as *jobs never opened* rather than pink slips. Which leads
straight to the group feeling it first.

---

## 2. The entry-level squeeze is now measured, not predicted

Last week's on-ramp story rested on a *job-ad* signal (entry roles asking for senior
judgment). This week it has **payroll data** behind it.

- A **Stanford** study using **ADP payroll records on 25 million workers** finds employment
  for workers **aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations fell ~13%** since late 2022 —
  while older and less-exposed workers held steady.
- For **early-career software developers** the drop is closer to **20%**.
- The mechanism matters: declines cluster where AI **automates** work, and are **muted where
  AI augments** it. The **Dallas Fed** reached a similar conclusion independently.

```
  EMPLOYMENT CHANGE SINCE LATE 2022 (AI-exposed roles)
  Junior software devs (22–25)  │███████████████████▼  ~ −20%
  All young workers (22–25)      │████████████▼         ~ −13%
  Older / less-exposed workers   │████████████████████  ~ flat
                                 └──────────────────────────────►
```

**Real example.** Two designers apply for the same junior seat. In 2022 the role existed
to do the *routine* production work — resizing, versioning, formatting — the exact layer AI
now drafts for free. In 2026 that seat wasn't posted at all; the team gave the routine work
to a tool and kept one *mid-level* person to steer it. The on-ramp didn't get harder — for
that role it **quietly disappeared.** The counter-move is the same as ever: don't compete on
the layer AI already owns; build the **validate-and-judge** layer the survivors are kept for.

## 3. The new gap: trained for *today's* AI, not tomorrow's job

The freshest finding of the week (released **28 July**) reframes the whole reskilling debate.

- The **Conference Board** reports that **most organisations are preparing workers for
  *today's* AI — not tomorrow's jobs.** Firms invest in **AI literacy and current-role
  upskilling**, but **few prepare workers for the deeper reskilling** an AI-transformed
  economy will demand.

Read alongside the layoff and entry-level data, the risk sharpens: companies are teaching
people to *operate this quarter's tools* while the ground under the *role itself* moves. For
an individual, the takeaway is blunt — **don't outsource your reskilling to your employer's
training budget.** Their horizon is this year's tool; yours has to be the next role.

---

## 4. Durable skills: the list got sharper and better sourced

Two fresh, independent lists this week converge on the same answer — the skills that don't
expire are the *human-judgment* ones.

- **LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise 2026:** **AI literacy is the #1 skill** — but it's defined
  as knowing *how AI generates answers, when outputs are wrong, and when human judgment is
  required.* And **soft skills take 7 of the top 10** spots: **conflict mitigation, public
  speaking, stakeholder management,** with **adaptability in the top three.**
- **Resume Now's "Resume of the Future" (28 Jul)** mined **O*NET** ability data for the
  cognitive skills valuable across hundreds of jobs: **communication, problem recognition,
  reasoning, flexible thinking, focus, and judgment.**
- The **AI Workforce Consortium** adds the flip side: **78% of ICT roles now embed AI
  technical skills** — AI fluency is becoming table stakes, so the *differentiator* moves
  back to the human layer on top of it.

The through-line with sections 1–3: as AI eats the routine floor, **the value migrates to the
judgment ceiling** — and "AI literacy" itself now means *judging the machine*, not just
operating it.

---

## 5. The counter-current still holds: people learn for identity, too

The "skilliday" story from earlier editions hasn't reversed — it's the human antidote running
in parallel. **~half of Europeans** plan to learn a skill on holiday and **37% have already
booked** one (Mastercard, 27,000 travellers / 28 countries); languages, cooking with local
chefs, and food-and-drink craft lead. Same instinct as the workplace shift: reach for
something **embodied, feedback-driven, and hard to automate.** Pottery and stakeholder
negotiation are closer cousins than they look.

## 🛠 The tool: the **Next-Role Reskill Ledger** (15 minutes, one page)

The Conference Board finding says employers train you for *now*. This tool makes you train
for *next*.

```
  1. NAME your role as an AI agent would describe it in one line.
       e.g. "I turn raw data into weekly dashboards."
  2. STRIP it: which part can AI already draft?  → that's your Track-B (at-risk) core.
  3. LIFT it: what's the JUDGMENT the routine was feeding?  → validate · decide · persuade.
  4. RESKILL LINE: pick ONE durable skill (critical thinking, a validation habit,
       stakeholder communication) + ONE next-tool skill. Not this quarter's tool — the
       one that eats your current tool.
  5. DATE it: 90-day review. Beat the skill half-life on purpose.
```

**Pair it with a free tool you already have.** Use any AI assistant as a **sparring partner,
not an oracle.** After you reach a conclusion, paste it back and prompt:
> *"Argue the strongest case that I'm wrong. List the three assumptions I didn't check."*

That one habit turns AI from an autopilot into a **critical-thinking gym** — the exact muscle
sections 1–4 say the market is repricing upward. (See `mindmap.mm` for the full picture and
`AI_Jobs_Scoreboard_2026.pptx` for a shareable doodle deck.)
