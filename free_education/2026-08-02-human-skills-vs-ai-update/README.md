## 2. Durable human skills — now with a body count at the entry level

The July brief argued durable skills were becoming the scarce asset. The August
data shows the *forcing function*: **the routine junior work that used to teach
those skills is being automated before newcomers can learn on it.**

- **Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index:** employment for software developers **aged
  22–25 fell ~20%** since 2024. For 22–25s across AI-exposed occupations, ~**−13%**
  since late 2022.
- **PwC:** AI-exposed entry-level roles are **7× more likely** to demand
  senior-level competencies (judgment, leadership). "Seniorised" junior roles grew
  **+35%** since 2019; conventional junior roles **−10%.**
- **Goldman Sachs (Apr 2026):** ~**16,000 net US job losses/month** attributable
  to AI (25k substituted − 9k augmented).
- **21%** of companies have already **frozen** entry-level hiring due to AI;
  another 15% expect to by year-end; nearly **half** expect to eliminate
  entry-level hiring by 2027.

> **Nuance that matters:** this is a **hiring** freeze more than a **firing**
> wave. AI is suppressing the *front door*, not mass-eliminating incumbents. The
> danger isn't "everyone gets replaced" — it's "no one gets the first rung."

**Real example.** A 2024-model junior analyst spent year one cleaning data and
reconciling spreadsheets — boring work, but it's *how you learn what "wrong"
looks like.* In 2026 an agent does that in seconds, and the job ad for the same
seat now reads *"must exercise judgment over AI outputs, present to stakeholders."*
The ladder didn't get taller — **the bottom two rungs were sawn off**, and
newcomers are asked to start at rung three. The winners are the ones who build
rung-three skills *before* they're hired.

## 🛠 A tool to try this week

**The "Rung-Three Résumé" test** — a 10-minute audit for anyone early-career (or
mentoring someone who is):

1. **Write** the job you want, then list the 3 tasks in it an AI agent can now do
   end-to-end. Cross them out — those won't be *your* value.
2. **For each crossed-out task, name the judgment call sitting on top of it:**
   *Is this output right? Is it wise? Should we act on it?* That's rung three.
3. **Score yourself 0–2 on each judgment call.** Zeros are your gap list.
4. **Pick one gap** and manufacture rung-three reps *before* you're hired: run a
   real dataset through an AI, then write the one-paragraph "here's what it got
   wrong and what I'd do instead." Do 5 of these — that folder *is* your portfolio.
5. **Pair with the habit:** ask any AI assistant (Claude, Copilot, Gemini) to
   argue the **opposite** of your conclusion. That single move converts an
   autopilot into a critical-thinking gym — the exact skill the +185% crowd is
   paying Coursera to teach them.

> Why this works: the market is no longer buying "I can produce the output."
> It's buying "I can be trusted to **validate** it." Manufacture that evidence.
